## 3. Mission, vision, purpose, and the human problem

**Mission (what Aturseva does today).** Connect the healthcare journey: help patients discover providers and book, join the right queue online or as a walk-in, attend consultations including video, reach their records, and keep follow-ups on track — while giving doctors and hospital teams coordinated workflows for the same journey. *(Grounded: File 02 matrix rows 1–14.)*

**Vision (the future Aturseva works toward).** A healthcare experience in which no patient feels lost, uninformed, or alone between the first booking and the last follow-up — and care teams feel supported rather than stretched.

**Purpose (why Aturseva exists).** Healthcare is a journey, and no one should walk it alone. The people are already caring; the journey just needs to hold together.

**The human problem Aturseva addresses.** A healthcare journey is fragmented for everyone in it: patients wait without knowing their turn, juggle paper and phone calls, and lose track of follow-up instructions; doctors and hospital teams coordinate across disconnected tools and desks. This is not anyone's failure — it is what unconnected journeys feel like. Aturseva exists to connect that journey, not to replace the people in it.

## 4. Brand positioning statement

> **For patients and the care teams who look after them — who need the healthcare journey to feel coordinated and understandable — Aturseva is a connected healthcare platform that brings appointments, queues, consultations, records, and follow-up into one calm, guided experience. Unlike a collection of separate tools for separate steps, Aturseva is deliberately designed around the whole journey and everyone in it: a helping hand that supports doctors' and hospital teams' work, never a replacement for it.**

**Positioning elements:**

| Element | Statement |
|---|---|
| Audience | Patients (primary) plus doctors, clinic/hospital teams (collaborators) |
| Need | A healthcare journey that is coordinated, understandable, and supportive |
| Category | Connected healthcare platform (patient app + provider software, one journey) |
| Core benefit | One calm, guided experience from first booking to last follow-up |
| Meaningful distinction | Journey-wide connection + human-first assistance (Sevika, doctor-approved) + support-not-replacement stance |
| Superiority claims | **None.** The distinction is a design intent, not a measured market comparison. |

## 5. Brand promise and supporting pillars

**Brand promise:**

> **Aturseva keeps your care journey connected — from the first booking to the last follow-up.**

**Pillars (each with practical implications):**

1. **Present through the journey.** Before, during, and after a visit — not one step, the whole arc.
   *Copy:* continuity language ("from… to…", "in step"); show sequences, not isolated features. *Visuals:* journey arcs, lanes, and timelines instead of disconnected feature tiles.
2. **Clarity over noise.** Patients get the information they need, in language they understand, when it matters.
   *Copy:* plain English, short sentences, no jargon, no hype. *Visuals:* monochrome restraint; one idea per frame; calm typography.
3. **Care guided by doctors.** Sevika's reminders and instructions follow doctor-approved plans.
   *Copy:* always attribute — "doctor-approved reminders", "supports the care plan set by your doctor". *Visuals:* approval attribution on every Sevika element.
4. **Connection, not replacement.** Doctors, reception, pharmacy, and lab teams are collaborators whose work Aturseva supports.
   *Copy:* "supports", "helps coordinate", "works alongside" — never "replaces", "automates away", "makes staff obsolete". *Visuals:* staff shown as active, respected participants in workflows.
5. **Respectful technology.** Technology serves the human moment; it is never the emotional centre of the story.
   *Copy:* lead with the person, then the product. *Visuals:* human-first cinematic moments first; interfaces revealed as support, not spectacle.

## 6. Audience relationship

**Patients are central.** All primary public copy speaks to the patient (or their carer) first. The voice is reassuring and calm: healthcare already carries enough anxiety; the brand adds orientation, not pressure.

| Audience | How the brand speaks to them | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Patients | Direct, warm, second person ("your appointment", "your place in the queue"). Sevika examples may mirror how they actually speak at home. | Primary audience for the public homepage. |
| Doctors | Professional peer tone. Respect for clinical authority is absolute — Aturseva supports their decisions and their follow-up plans. Focus: schedule, unified queue, patient context, follow-up coordination. *(File 02 rows 6, 13)* | Addressed mainly on the doctors' pathway/section. |
| Reception / front-desk teams | Operational partner tone. QR-based and assisted registration make walk-ins smoother — staff remain in charge. *(File 02 row 2)* | Never framed as being "replaced" by QR self-service. |
| Assistant doctors | Coordination-support tone; their role in the consultation workflow is acknowledged, not diminished. | Brief, workflow-context mentions. |
| Clinic / hospital administrators | Clear, operational tone: one connected system, shared queues across online and walk-in, operational visibility. *(File 02 row 7)* | Emphasise "visibility" and "coordination", not surveillance language. |
| Pharmacy & laboratory teams | Workflow-support tone; connected workflows where the product enables them. *(File 02 row 7)* | No implication of integration with every external pharmacy/lab. |

**Standing rule:** patients are the entry point of the story; provider teams appear as collaborators *in the same journey*, never as opponents or obstacles.

## 8. Tone-of-voice rules

### 8.1 Headlines
Short, human, journey-framed. One idea.
- ✅ "Healthcare is a journey."
- ✅ "Care that continues at home."
- ❌ "Revolutionising Care Coordination Through Intelligent Digital Infrastructure"

### 8.2 Explanatory copy
Plain sentences; concrete nouns; benefit before mechanism.
- ✅ "Join the queue from your phone — and see your token move."
- ❌ "Leveraging our proprietary queue-management engine to dynamically tokenise patient flows."

### 8.3 CTAs (buttons and links)
Honest verbs; no dark patterns, no fake urgency or scarcity.
- ✅ "Begin the journey" · "See how it connects" · "Get started"
- ❌ "GET STARTED NOW — LIMITED SLOTS!" · "Doctors are waiting!"

### 8.4 Product UI labels (inside representative previews)
Quiet and functional; medical attribution where relevant.
- ✅ "Queue position" · "Doctor-approved reminder" · "Follow-up suggested by your doctor"
- ❌ "AI-powered smart-queue turbo mode"

### 8.5 Sensitive healthcare situations
Empathy first; information second; never promise outcomes; never frighten, never trivialise. Medication and follow-up contexts always attribute to the doctor's plan.
- ✅ "A gentle reminder from the plan your doctor set: your 2 pm medication."
- ❌ "You forgot your medicine — your health is at risk!"
- ❌ "Never miss a dose again — guaranteed."
- Standing rule: no fear-based marketing of any kind.

## 9. Sevika brand role

**What Sevika is:** the patient's supportive companion inside the patient app — an existing, working part of the product (File 02 §4) that helps patients *navigate* their care: booking help, queue notifications, doctor-approved medication reminders, doctor-approved diet/care instructions, follow-up reminders, and authorised next-appointment booking.

**Brand warmth:** Sevika speaks like a caring, patient helper — unhurried, encouraging, clear. Warmth is the point; Sevika is the most human voice in the product.

**Hard boundaries (mirror of File 02 §4.3):** Sevika never diagnoses, prescribes, changes doses, overrides doctors, or makes independent medical decisions. Every reminder and instruction is attributed to a doctor-approved plan. Sevika is an assistant, never a doctor or nurse replacement.

**Intentional Hinglish example snippets** — permitted *only* inside a clearly labelled illustrative mock conversation demonstrating multilingual patient interaction (File 02 §4.4). The surrounding site copy stays English.

> **Illustrative example — representative Sevika conversation (not a live chat):**
> - Sevika: "Namaste! Aap ki appointment book ho gayi hai. Kal subah 10 baje, Dr. Sharma ke clinic — aap ki jagah queue mein #12 hai. Update aata rahega." *(Hello! Your appointment is booked. Tomorrow 10 am at Dr. Sharma's clinic — your queue position is #12. Updates will keep coming.)*
> - Sevika: "Yeh reminder aap ke doctor ne approve kiya hai: aaj dopahar 2 baje wali dawai lena hai." *(This reminder was approved by your doctor: take your 2 pm medication today.)*
> - Sevika: "Aap ka follow-up agle hafte hai. Book karne mein madad chahiye?" *(Your follow-up is next week. Need help booking it?)*

**Spill rule:** Hinglish must never appear in navigation, headings, CTAs, feature descriptions, or any general marketing copy — only inside the example itself, clearly contained.

## 10. Core narrative arc for the landing page

Aligned with File 02 §11.6 (required story order) — and with the live homepage's existing structure:

1. **The human journey opens the story.** Healthcare is a journey; no one should walk it alone. Emotion first, no product yet.
2. **How the journeys connect.** Patient, doctor, hospital — three separate paths shown converging into one coordinated flow. This is the signature visual moment.
3. **Representative product reveal.** The connected experience made visible through a clearly labelled representative interface (booking → queue → consultation → records). Never presented as a live screenshot or data feed.
4. **Sevika's ongoing support.** Care continues after the visit: doctor-approved reminders, follow-ups, and authorised rebooking — the journey's quiet companion.
5. **Pathways.** Three doors close the page: patients, doctors, hospitals — each invited into the same journey in their own way.

## 12. Claims and language guardrails

### 12.1 Allowed patterns
- Explain working capabilities plainly ("patients can book", "doctors manage one unified queue").
- Describe the product as one connected journey across patient app and provider software.
- Describe Sevika as supportive and doctor-guided, with attributed reminders.
- Use clearly labelled representative UI ("Representative preview") to explain workflows.
- Speak of the philosophy: support before, during, and after a visit; care and coordination, not replacement.

### 12.2 Prohibited claims (without new, explicit owner evidence)
- **Invented numbers** of any kind: patients served, doctors onboarded, hospitals, downloads, savings, queue-time reductions, retention, accuracy, success rates.
- **Outcomes**: guaranteed diagnosis, cure, recovery, zero waiting, "never miss a dose".
- **Autonomous medical AI**: AI diagnosis, AI prescriptions, AI dose changes, clinical accuracy percentages.
- **Replacement framing**: replacing doctors, nurses, receptionists, hospitals, pharmacies, labs — in any phrasing.
- **Social proof**: testimonials, customer/partner names, logos, awards, accreditations.
- **Compliance claims**: HIPAA or any regulatory/compliance status — unless the owner verifies exact status and scope.
- **Universal integrations**: "works with every lab/pharmacy/hospital system".
- **Commercial invention**: pricing, plan names, trials, launch dates.
- **Market disparagement**: naming or diminishing competitors; claiming market leadership or "first/only" positions.

## 13. Brand differentiation (credible, non-disparaging)

Aturseva differentiates on **a human-first coordination story**, told through choices any reader can verify on the site itself:

1. **Journey-wide by design.** The product is deliberately built around the entire journey — booking, queue, consultation, records, follow-up — not a single step sold as a point solution. *(Self-referential design intent; no claim about what others do.)*
2. **Everyone in the journey is in the story.** Patients, doctors, and hospital teams appear inside one narrative — including walk-in patients without smartphones. Inclusion is the differentiator, not feature counts.
3. **Assistance with boundaries, stated openly.** Sevika helps and reminds along doctor-approved plans; the brand says so explicitly, including what Sevika does not do. Restraint, honestly stated, is itself a differentiator in a hype-heavy category.
4. **Calm monochrome craft.** The monochrome visual language signals seriousness and restraint; it deliberately refuses the visual noise of generic SaaS marketing.

**Rules:** no competitor names, no "unlike other apps", no invented market research, no leadership claims. Differentiation is demonstrated, not asserted.

## 15. Practical brand checklist (for writers & designers)

Run this before approving any page copy or visual:

1. Is all general copy polished, natural English (Hinglish only inside a labelled Sevika example)?
2. Is every capability claim traceable to a File 02 matrix row?
3. Are Sevika reminders/instructions attributed to doctor-approved plans?
4. Any statistic, testimonial, partner, award, or compliance claim present? (Must be none.)
5. Any replacement framing ("instead of doctors/staff/reception") present? (Must be none.)
6. Any promise of outcomes, zero waiting, or clinical accuracy present? (Must be none.)
7. Is pricing/plan detail absent (unless the owner has supplied it)?
8. Is the visual strictly monochrome — black, white, grey; no teal/green/gold/warm tints, no coloured gradients?
9. Does the piece lead with the human moment, with technology as support?
10. Are workflows shown as **connected sequences** (not disconnected feature tiles)?
11. Are representative interfaces clearly labelled ("Representative preview"), never implied as live data?
12. Is the patient the entry point, with provider teams shown as collaborators?
13. Are CTAs honest verbs without fake urgency?
14. In sensitive contexts (medication, follow-up), is the tone empathetic and doctor-attributed?
15. Does the piece keep the story order where applicable (journey → connection → reveal → Sevika → pathways)?

Any "no" on items 2–7, or any unresolved checklist failure, blocks the piece.
